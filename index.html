🔍 What dissect Actually Does

Every video that does well online has a structure hiding inside it. A hook in the first three seconds. A certain pace of cuts. A specific way the story is told. A caption style. A sound choice. Most people watch those videos and feel something, but they cannot say why the video worked.

dissect takes the guesswork out of that. You give it a link to a video, and it works through the video piece by piece. It listens to what is being said, notes how the video is edited, tracks how the information is laid out, and writes all of it down in plain language. The result is a blueprint: a written recipe that describes how that video was built.

Because the blueprint is written down, it becomes reusable. You can follow it in a different topic, in a different niche, with your own face, your own voice and your own footage. Instead of copying a video pixel for pixel, you copy the thinking behind it. That is the whole idea behind dissect.

✨ What You Get From Every Video

  • A hook breakdown. What happens in the first few seconds, what is said, and why a viewer would keep watching instead of scrolling.
  • A full transcript of the audio. dissect listens to the spoken words and writes them out, so you can quote, remix or rewrite them.
  • A structure map. The order of the beats: setup, build, twist, payoff, call to action — whatever pattern the video is actually using.
  • A pacing and edit report. How fast it moves, where the cuts land, how long each section holds attention.
  • On-screen and caption notes. What text appears on screen, how it is positioned, and how it supports the spoken words.
  • A reusable blueprint document. The final output: a clear, tidy file you can read, edit, save and come back to.

🧭 Using dissect, Step by Step

The flow below is the same flow you will use every time. Nothing about it is technical — it is closer to briefing a very patient assistant than to running software.

  1. Pick a video that is already working. Choose something in your niche with views you would like to have. Popular videos are best, because the blueprint you get back describes a pattern that has already proven it can hold attention.
  2. Copy the link. From YouTube, TikTok, Reels or X — whichever platform the video lives on. The share button gives you a clean link.
  3. Ask Claude Code to dissect it. Paste the link into the chat and say what you want out of it. You can be as vague or as specific as you like.
  4. Wait for the blueprint. dissect steps through the video in order. Longer videos take longer, and a very long video can take a few minutes.
  5. Read the blueprint out loud. This sounds odd, but it is the fastest way to spot the pattern. Once you can hear the structure, you can plan a version of your own.
  6. Make your video using the blueprint. Swap in your topic, your expertise and your footage. Keep the pacing and the hook style, change everything else.

💡 Tips for Better Blueprints

  • Dissect more than one video at a time. Three or four videos from the same creator or the same niche will show you the repeating pattern, not just a single lucky hit.
  • Be specific about your goal. Saying "I want to make a 30-second TikTok in this style" gets you a more useful blueprint than a general request.
  • Ask follow-up questions. The chat does not end when the blueprint is written. You can ask it to rewrite the blueprint for a different audience, shorten it, or turn it into a shot list.
  • Save your blueprints. Keep the good ones in a folder. After a few weeks you will have a personal library of patterns that you know work for your audience.
  • Treat the blueprint as a starting point. The best results come from following the structure and then bringing your own voice to it.

🛠️ If Something Goes Wrong

The link will not process

Some videos are private, region-locked, or age-restricted. Open the video in your browser first. If it plays without you signing in, it should dissect fine.

It is taking a long time

Long videos with a lot of talking take the longest, because every spoken word has to be transcribed. A ten-minute video is quick. A two-hour podcast is not. Start with something short to get a feel for it.

You cannot find the skill after installing it

Close Claude Code completely and open it again. Skills are read when a session starts, so a fresh session usually fixes it. If it is still missing, check that the dissect folder sits in the same place as your other skills.

The transcript is missing words

Background music, heavy accents and overlapping speakers all make transcription harder. It is usually good enough to read the structure, which is what the blueprint really depends on.
